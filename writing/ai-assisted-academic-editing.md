# AI 辅助学术文本编辑与作者声音规范 / AI-assisted Academic Editing and Authorial Voice Standard

本规范用于论文、综述、基金申请、研究报告、学术邮件及其他正式科研文本的 AI 辅助编辑。目标是提高文本的准确性、清晰度、连贯性和作者声音的一致性，使最终文本真实反映作者的研究、判断与表达；不得将编辑过程用于伪装作者身份、隐瞒应披露的 AI 参与，或规避 AI 检测。

This standard governs AI-assisted editing of manuscripts, reviews, grant proposals, research reports, academic correspondence, and other formal research writing. Its purpose is to improve accuracy, clarity, coherence, and consistency with the author's voice so that the final text genuinely reflects the author's research, judgment, and expression. Editing must not be used to misrepresent authorship, conceal AI assistance when disclosure is required, or evade AI-detection systems.

## 中文版

### 1. 核心原则

- **先保证科学内容，再优化语言。** 不得为了语言流畅而改变研究事实、实验条件、统计结果、证据强度或结论边界。
- **作者声音优先。** 以作者实际的研究逻辑、术语习惯、论证方式和已确认的写作偏好为依据，不套用统一的“AI 学术腔”。
- **自然编辑，不做检测规避。** 可以消除空泛、重复、模板化和不自然的表达，但不得以降低 AI 检测分数、绕过检测器或隐藏 AI 参与为目标。
- **保留可追溯性。** 保留原稿、关键修订记录和必要的 AI 使用记录；遵守目标期刊、学校、基金机构和出版平台的披露要求。
- **不以检测器判定作者身份。** AI 文本检测结果存在不确定性，不应单独作为认定作者身份、学术不端或文本真实性的依据。

### 2. 可执行的文本编辑方法

#### 2.1 建立作者声音参考

- 优先使用作者本人已发表或已确认的英文论文作为风格参考。
- 可归纳作者偏好的句长、段落组织、术语、主动/被动语态、论证节奏和表达正式程度。
- 只模仿用户本人明确授权的写作样本；不得未经许可模仿其他研究者的独特表达或将他人文本伪装成作者原创。
- 风格参考只用于语言一致性，不得复制参考文本中的具体句子、论点或未核实事实。

#### 2.2 删除空泛和模板化表达

检查并在确无信息损失时修改：

- 没有明确科学含义的开场句、总结句和过渡句。
- 重复表达同一结果或观点的句子。
- 诸如“近年来受到广泛关注”“具有重要意义”“提供了新的见解”等缺少具体对象或证据的套话。
- 过度使用的固定连接词、机械式段首句和重复的段落结构。
- 与研究问题无关的背景介绍、夸大意义和不必要的形容词。
- 不符合作者实际研究内容的泛化陈述。

不得为了改变文本的检测特征而机械替换同义词。每次修改都必须改善准确性、逻辑、可读性或信息密度。

#### 2.3 调整句子与段落结构

- 根据论证逻辑调整句子顺序，而不是对每个句子做表面改写。
- 长句在包含多个独立主张或多个逻辑层次时可以拆分；短句在关系紧密时可以合并。
- 句长应由内容复杂度和阅读节奏决定，不刻意追求长短句比例或人为制造句式变化。
- 避免连续段落使用完全相同的“背景—结果—意义”模板。
- 每段应围绕一个明确中心，按“主张—证据—解释—限制/意义”的实际需要组织。
- 保留必要的学术限定语，不能为了简洁而删除“可能”“在本研究条件下”“与……相关”等影响结论强度的词语。

#### 2.4 增加具体性与研究内容的一致性

- 优先使用真实的物种、材料、组织、发育阶段、处理条件、基因/蛋白名称、数据来源和实验结果。
- 将泛泛的描述改为能够由数据或文献直接支持的具体表述。
- 讨论部分应明确区分本研究观察到的结果、已有文献证据、合理解释和待验证假说。
- 不得为了让文本显得“更像人写的”而添加个人经历、实验观察、研究动机或未经记录的细节。
- 不能从图表或摘要推断原始数据中不存在的精确数值、机制或因果关系。

#### 2.5 保留作者的科学判断

- 对研究假说、结果解释、局限性和创新性表述，优先询问或依据作者已确认的科学判断。
- 对多个可能解释，应呈现证据支持程度，不擅自替作者选择最强结论。
- 对证据不足的句子，标注待确认或降低措辞强度，不通过润色把推测写成事实。
- 结论必须与研究设计、样本量、统计分析和实验验证范围相匹配。

### 3. Humanizer 工具的使用边界

AI Humanizer 或类似改写工具可能通过词语替换、句式重排、段落重写等方式改变 AI 生成文本的表面风格。此类工具应按编辑工具而不是“隐身工具”评估。

**允许的用途：**

- 在符合期刊和机构政策的前提下，帮助改善清晰度、语气、语法、可读性和作者声音一致性。
- 将生硬、重复或过度模板化的句子改为更准确、自然的学术表达。
- 根据作者提供的真实写作样本，统一术语和表达风格。
- 对修改内容进行逐句核对，确保含义、数据、引文和结论没有改变。

**不允许的用途：**

- 以“绕过 AI 检测”“降低 AI 检测率”“使 AI 文本无法识别”或类似目标配置工具或提示词。
- 根据 AI 检测器的分数反复改写文本，直到被判定为人类撰写。
- 使用同义词替换、语序扰动、刻意制造语法错误或其他文本变形来伪装人工创作。
- 隐瞒期刊、学校、资助机构或其他规则要求披露的 AI 使用情况。
- 将 AI 生成的研究内容、解释、分析或未经核实的事实伪装成作者亲自完成或验证的工作。

### 4. 科学内容与事实保真检查

每次实质性改写后，必须检查：

- 基因、蛋白、物种、品种、组织、发育阶段和处理名称是否准确。
- 数值、单位、样本量、重复数、统计检验、P 值、阈值和效应方向是否与原始记录一致。
- 引文是否真实存在，且确实支持相应语句；遵循 `reference-verification.md`。
- 相关性是否被错误改写为因果性，预测是否被错误改写为实验验证。
- 研究结果是否被夸大，限制条件是否被遗漏。
- 图表、补充材料、Methods、Results、Discussion 和 Abstract 之间是否一致。
- 改写是否引入原文没有的新主张、新数据或新的参考文献。

对无法核实的内容使用明确标记，例如 `[TO BE CONFIRMED]`，并列入待确认事项。

### 5. 推荐编辑工作流

1. **理解原稿：** 明确研究问题、核心结果、作者意图和目标期刊要求。
2. **建立风格参考：** 如有作者本人确认的写作样本，提取术语与表达偏好。
3. **诊断文本问题：** 标出逻辑跳跃、重复、空泛、语法错误、术语不一致和证据不足之处。
4. **实质性编辑：** 优先调整论证结构和信息表达，再进行句子层面的润色。
5. **科学核查：** 对照数据、脚本、图表、文献和实验记录核对所有实质性修改。
6. **作者复核：** 将涉及科学解释、创新性、贡献和结论强度的修改交由作者确认。
7. **披露与归档：** 按目标期刊或机构要求披露 AI 辅助情况，并保存原稿及必要修订记录。

### 6. 推荐使用的编辑指令

以下指令用于质量编辑，而不是规避检测：

> 请以学术编辑的身份修改以下文本。保留原作者的科学观点、研究逻辑、数据、术语、引用和结论强度。优先修复逻辑跳跃、重复表达、空泛套话、语法问题和不自然的句子结构，使语言准确、清晰、简洁，并与作者提供的真实写作样本保持一致。不要添加未经证实的事实、实验细节、文献或解释。不要为了改变 AI 检测结果而改写，也不要承诺或追求任何检测器分数。对含义不清或证据不足的内容，请标记并解释，而不是自行猜测。

### 7. 交付前检查清单

- [ ] 文本准确表达作者真实的研究问题、分析和结论。
- [ ] 语言符合作者本人确认的写作习惯和目标期刊风格。
- [ ] 已删除无信息价值的重复、空泛和模板化表达。
- [ ] 句子与段落结构服务于逻辑，不是为了制造人为的风格变化。
- [ ] 没有添加未经证实的实验细节、个人经历、数据或参考文献。
- [ ] 所有数字、术语、引文和结论均已核实。
- [ ] 相关性、因果性、预测和实验验证之间的界限准确。
- [ ] 已保留必要的 AI 使用记录，并遵守期刊或机构披露政策。
- [ ] 没有以规避 AI 检测或隐瞒 AI 参与为目标进行改写。
- [ ] 作者已复核涉及科学判断和研究结论的实质性修改。

**核心原则：让文本更准确、更清晰、更符合作者真实声音，而不是让 AI 生成内容伪装成未经 AI 辅助的作品。**

## English Version

### 1. Core Principles

- **Scientific fidelity comes before stylistic polish.** Never change research facts, experimental conditions, statistical results, evidential strength, or the scope of conclusions to make prose sound smoother.
- **Prioritize the author's voice.** Use the author's actual reasoning, terminology, argumentation patterns, and confirmed writing preferences. Do not impose a generic AI-style academic voice.
- **Edit naturally; do not evade detection.** Remove vague, repetitive, formulaic, or awkward language when doing so improves the text. Do not optimize revisions to lower AI-detection scores, bypass detectors, or conceal AI involvement.
- **Preserve traceability.** Retain the original draft, material revision records, and necessary AI-use records. Follow the disclosure requirements of the target journal, institution, funder, and publisher.
- **Do not treat detector output as proof of authorship.** AI-text detection is uncertain and should not be used alone to determine authorship, misconduct, or textual authenticity.

### 2. Practical Editing Methods

#### 2.1 Establish an Author-Voice Reference

- Prefer the author's own published or author-approved English papers as style references.
- Identify preferences in sentence length, paragraph organization, terminology, active/passive voice, argumentative rhythm, and level of formality.
- Use only writing samples that the user has authored or explicitly authorized. Do not imitate another researcher's distinctive style without permission or misrepresent another person's text as the author's original writing.
- Use style references only for consistency. Do not copy specific sentences, arguments, or unverified facts from them.

#### 2.2 Remove Vague and Formulaic Language

Review and revise the following when doing so does not remove meaningful information:

- Opening, concluding, or transitional sentences that carry no clear scientific meaning.
- Sentences that repeat the same result or argument.
- Generic phrases such as “has attracted considerable attention,” “is of great significance,” or “provides new insights” when no specific subject or evidence is supplied.
- Overused stock transitions, mechanical topic sentences, and repetitive paragraph structures.
- Background material unrelated to the research question, exaggerated significance, and unnecessary adjectives.
- Generalized statements that do not reflect the actual study.

Do not perform mechanical synonym substitution to change detection characteristics. Every revision must improve accuracy, logic, readability, or information density.

#### 2.3 Improve Sentence and Paragraph Structure

- Reorder sentences according to the argument rather than merely paraphrasing each sentence.
- Split long sentences when they contain multiple independent claims or layers of reasoning; combine short sentences when their relationship is close.
- Let sentence length follow content complexity and reading rhythm. Do not target an artificial long/short sentence ratio or manufacture variation.
- Avoid repeating an identical “background–result–significance” template across consecutive paragraphs.
- Organize each paragraph around a clear central point, using a claim–evidence–interpretation–limitation/significance structure as appropriate.
- Preserve scientifically important qualifiers. Do not remove terms such as “may,” “under the conditions tested,” or “was associated with” when they determine the strength or scope of a claim.

#### 2.4 Increase Specificity and Consistency with the Actual Study

- Prefer accurate names of species, materials, tissues, developmental stages, treatments, genes/proteins, data sources, and experimental results.
- Replace vague descriptions with specific statements directly supported by data or literature.
- In the Discussion, distinguish observations from the present study, evidence from previous literature, plausible interpretations, and hypotheses requiring further validation.
- Do not add personal experiences, experimental observations, motivations, or undocumented details to make text appear more human-written.
- Do not infer precise values, mechanisms, or causal relationships that are absent from the underlying data.

#### 2.5 Preserve the Author's Scientific Judgment

- For hypotheses, interpretation of results, limitations, and claims of novelty, prioritize the author's confirmed scientific judgment.
- When multiple explanations are possible, represent the strength of evidence rather than choosing the strongest conclusion on the author's behalf.
- Flag unsupported statements for confirmation or reduce their strength. Do not polish speculation into fact.
- Ensure that conclusions match the study design, sample size, statistical analysis, and scope of experimental validation.

### 3. Boundaries for Humanizer Tools

AI humanizers and similar rewriting tools may change the surface style of generated text through word substitution, sentence reordering, and paragraph rewriting. They should be evaluated as editing aids, not as tools for hiding AI involvement.

**Permitted uses:**

- When consistent with journal and institutional policies, improve clarity, tone, grammar, readability, and consistency with the author's voice.
- Revise stiff, repetitive, or overly formulaic sentences into more accurate and natural academic prose.
- Use genuine writing samples supplied by the author to maintain terminology and stylistic consistency.
- Review revisions sentence by sentence to ensure that meaning, data, citations, and conclusions remain unchanged.

**Prohibited uses:**

- Configure tools or prompts with goals such as “bypass AI detection,” “lower the AI-detection rate,” “make AI text undetectable,” or equivalent objectives.
- Repeatedly rewrite text based on detector scores until it is classified as human-written.
- Use synonym spinning, deliberate word-order perturbation, intentional grammatical errors, or other text distortions to disguise machine authorship.
- Conceal AI use when disclosure is required by a journal, institution, funder, or other applicable policy.
- Present AI-generated research content, interpretations, analyses, or unverified facts as work personally performed or validated by the author.

### 4. Scientific and Factual Fidelity Checks

After every substantive rewrite, verify:

- Gene, protein, species, cultivar, tissue, developmental-stage, and treatment names.
- Numerical values, units, sample sizes, replicate counts, statistical tests, P values, thresholds, and effect directions against original records.
- Whether each cited reference genuinely exists and supports the associated statement; follow `reference-verification.md`.
- Whether correlation has been incorrectly rewritten as causation, or prediction as experimental validation.
- Whether results have been exaggerated or limitations omitted.
- Consistency among figures, supplementary materials, Methods, Results, Discussion, and Abstract.
- Whether the rewrite introduces claims, data, or references absent from the original material.

Mark unverifiable content explicitly, for example with `[TO BE CONFIRMED]`, and list it for follow-up.

### 5. Recommended Editing Workflow

1. **Understand the draft:** Identify the research question, key results, author's intent, and target-journal requirements.
2. **Establish a style reference:** If author-approved writing samples are available, extract terminology and stylistic preferences.
3. **Diagnose text problems:** Identify logical gaps, repetition, vagueness, grammatical errors, inconsistent terminology, and unsupported claims.
4. **Perform substantive editing:** Improve argument structure and information flow before sentence-level polishing.
5. **Verify scientific content:** Check all substantive changes against data, scripts, figures, literature, and experimental records.
6. **Obtain author review:** Ask the author to confirm revisions affecting scientific interpretation, novelty, contribution, and conclusion strength.
7. **Disclose and archive:** Disclose AI assistance according to journal or institutional policy and retain the original draft and necessary revision records.

### 6. Recommended Editing Instruction

The following instruction is intended for quality editing, not detection evasion:

> Act as an academic editor. Preserve the author's scientific claims, research logic, data, terminology, citations, and strength of conclusions. Prioritize correcting logical gaps, repetition, vague boilerplate, grammatical problems, and awkward sentence structures. Make the prose accurate, clear, concise, and consistent with the author's genuine writing samples. Do not add unverified facts, experimental details, references, or interpretations. Do not rewrite text to alter AI-detection results, and do not promise or optimize for any detector score. Flag and explain ambiguous or unsupported content instead of guessing.

### 7. Pre-delivery Checklist

- [ ] The text accurately represents the author's actual research questions, analyses, and conclusions.
- [ ] The language is consistent with the author's confirmed writing preferences and target-journal style.
- [ ] Repetitive, vague, and formulaic wording without informational value has been removed.
- [ ] Sentence and paragraph structures serve the argument rather than manufacturing artificial stylistic variation.
- [ ] No unverified experimental details, personal experiences, data, or references have been added.
- [ ] All numbers, terminology, citations, and conclusions have been verified.
- [ ] The distinctions among association, causation, prediction, and experimental validation are accurate.
- [ ] Necessary AI-use records have been retained and journal/institutional disclosure policies followed.
- [ ] No rewriting has been performed to evade AI detection or conceal AI involvement.
- [ ] The author has reviewed substantive changes involving scientific judgment and conclusions.

**Core principle: make the text more accurate, clearer, and more consistent with the author's genuine voice—not make AI-generated content masquerade as work produced without AI assistance.**
